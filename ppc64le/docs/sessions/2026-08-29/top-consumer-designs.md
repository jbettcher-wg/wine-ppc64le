# Part A — Serving Cyberpunk's PeekMessageW storm guest-side

## A.0 What exists today (verified by reading, file paths exact)

The fast path is generated by `wine-upstream/tools/spec2thunk/spec2thunk`, `emit_fast_body`,
`kind == 'peek'` (~line 2064). Mechanism: user32's `PeekMessageW` export points at a fast
body appended after the trap-stub array; a call that cannot be served falls through to the
original trap stub with all argument registers intact. The body answers **exactly one shape,
and only with "empty"**:

1. `gs:0x198` (TEB `SystemReserved1[1]`) non-null — seeded by
   `dlls/win32u/winstation.c:seed_guest_peek_queue()` (line ~247) on the thread's first
   `get_shared_queue()` lookup; low bit = sabotage tag.
2. `hwnd == NULL`, `first == 0`, `last == 0`, and `HIWORD(flags) == 0` (no `PM_QS_*`).
3. Force-a-real-trap pacing: whenever the KUSER_SHARED_DATA tick (0x7ffe0320) moves, and at
   latest every 256 fast answers (per-thread budget at `gs:0x1a4`, last tick at `gs:0x1a0`) —
   this is what keeps `check_for_driver_events()` (the X11 input pump),
   `flush_window_surfaces()`, and the server's `access_time` hung-queue guard alive.
4. The seqlock-consistent read of the thread's `queue_shm`
   (`include/wine/server_protocol.h`, `queue_shm_t` at +16 past the shared object's
   seq/id): `wake_mask == 0 && changed_mask == 0 && !(wake_bits & 0x1cff) &&
   !(changed_bits & 0x1dbf)`, seq even and unchanged across the read.

Anything else → the trap it always was. Kill switch `WINE_PPC64LE_NO_PEEK_BYPASS=1`;
negative control `WINE_PPC64LE_PEEK_SABOTAGE=1`; gate
`ppc64le/cpu/check-peek-fastpath.sh` + `ppc64le/cpu/probes/peek_fastpath.c`.

`user32.PeekMessageW` is a bare `return NtUserPeekMessage(...)` (`dlls/user32/message.c:710`),
so the guest body is mirroring the right contract with nothing in between.

## A.1 Why Cyberpunk's calls miss it — what is known vs not

**A correction to the report's inference first.** `frame-cost-budget.md` §2 reads
flat==syscall equality as "none of CP2077's peeks are served." Strictly, TRAP_STATS counts
only *trapping* calls; a fast-path answer produces neither a trap nor a syscall and is
invisible to both counters. What the equality proves is that every one of the 247,802/s
*trapping* peeks did a full syscall. Whether an additional invisible population is being
served fast doesn't change the budget (the 247.8k/s that trap are the 12 ms), but it changes
the *diagnosis*: 247.8k/s of traps could be

* (H-shape) calls whose `(hwnd, first, last, flags)` fail the shape test — non-null hwnd,
  a ranged filter, or a `PM_QS_*` word;
* (H-state) calls of the right shape that fail the *state* test — a stuck queue bit
  (`QS_RAWINPUT`/`QS_PAINT`/`QS_TIMER` living in `wake_bits`), or nonzero shm masks left by
  an interleaved `MsgWaitForMultipleObjects`;
* (H-seed) a seeding failure in this process (gs:0x198 null or clobbered on the GameThread),
  making every call trap regardless of shape. The probe proves seeding works in a small
  process, not in CP2077 with an overlay and 18 threads named GameThread.

These have different fixes (extend the body / fix a bit-source or accept / fix the seed), so
**the capture in A.2 is the gating step, not a formality.** The report's step 1 said the
same; this design specifies it fully.

## A.2 Step 1 — the shape-and-state capture (gating measurement, ~half a day)

Temporary, env-gated instrumentation in `dlls/win32u/message.c:NtUserPeekMessage` (native
side — one ERR-class printf path, no guest rebuild, no FEX involvement):

* Gate: `WINE_PPC64LE_PEEK_SHAPE=N` (log first N calls verbatim, then aggregate).
* Per call, record: `(hwnd != 0, first, last, flags)`, the return value (message vs empty),
  and — read under one `get_shared_queue` seqlock pass — `wake_mask, wake_bits,
  changed_mask, changed_bits`, plus `NtCurrentTeb()->SystemReserved1[1]`
  (seeded/sabotage/null — win32u wrote it, win32u can read it back).
* Aggregate: histogram keyed by shape tuple; per-shape counts of {empty, message-returned};
  per-shape counts of "would the generalized predicate of A.4 have answered empty"
  (compute it in C right there — this directly measures the serve fraction *f* before any
  guest assembly is written); a bits-nonzero breakdown by bit.
* Emit the histogram every ~10 s to the log. One benchmark leg, owner-run:
  the standard instrumented launch of `frame-cost-budget.md` §2 with
  `WINE_PPC64LE_PEEK_SHAPE=200`.

Decision table:

| capture says | it means | next |
|---|---|---|
| shapes are null-filter but seed slot null on GameThread | H-seed | fix seeding (bug hunt in this tree), fast body already sufficient |
| shapes non-null-filter (hwnd/range/PM_QS), predicate-empty would serve ≥90% | H-shape | build A.4 exactly as designed |
| right shapes, but bits/masks nonzero on most calls | H-state | name the stuck bit's source first; A.4 alone recovers little |
| large message-returned fraction (>10%) | queue genuinely busy | fast path caps out at the empty fraction; re-derive the win |

## A.3 What state a guest-side peek needs, and what queue_shm can and cannot answer

A peek has two possible answers. They are not symmetric.

**"Empty" needs only proof of absence**, and `queue_shm` carries exactly that proof:
`wake_bits`/`changed_bits` are the server-maintained summary of everything pending
(posted, sent, timer, paint, hardware input classes), and `wake_mask`/`changed_mask` are the
receiver's own last-armed wait masks. Wine's native no-server fast path —
`check_queue_bits()` (`dlls/win32u/message.c:2920`) — answers "empty" from precisely these
four fields, **and it never looks at `hwnd`, `first`, or `last`.** That is the load-bearing
observation for generalization: *queue-global quiet implies empty for every filter*. If no
bit of the filter's signal class is set anywhere in the queue, then no message matching any
window or range within that class exists. Filtered peeks are answerable-when-quiet; they are
only *unanswerable* when bits are set but possibly by messages outside the filter — and in
that case native also goes to the server, so falling to the trap is behavior-identical.

The mask precondition is simpler than it looks: for every `NtUserPeekMessage` call,
`filter->mask == 0`, so the required shm masks are `(0, 0)` for *all* shapes — the `PM_QS_*`
word changes only which bits must be quiet (`signal_bits`/`clear_bits`), not the masks. An
interleaved `MsgWaitForMultipleObjectsEx` leaves nonzero masks in shm, which refuses the fast
answer until the next real peek's server `get_message` resets them
(`server/queue.c:3381` writes `shared->wake_mask/changed_mask` from the request; the
satisfied-wait path clears them at `server/queue.c:305`). Cost: one trap per wait cycle.
Self-healing, no design needed.

**"Here is a message" needs the message itself** — contents, removal (a server-side state
mutation), sent-message dispatch, hook invocation (`WH_GETMESSAGE`), `msg->pt` DPI mapping,
`thread_info->message_time` bookkeeping. All of that is server- or native-owned. A guest-side
message store mirroring the server queue would be a second copy of the queue's truth with
every race that implies. **Message-returning peeks are fundamentally not guest-answerable and
stay traps, permanently.** This is fine: the storm is a poll storm; the capture will measure
the empty fraction, expected ≳99.9% (16,635 peeks per window-frame cannot be 16,635
messages).

This asymmetry gives the design its central safety property: **an empty-only fast path can
never drop, duplicate, or reorder a message, because it never touches one.** Its entire error
surface is one-sided — answering "empty" when it should have trapped. Every hazard analysis
below reduces to bounding that one wrong answer.

## A.4 The design: generalize the fast body to mirror check_queue_bits for all shapes

**Extend, don't restructure.** Argument:

* The restructure candidates fail on inspection. (a) A guest-side mirrored message queue —
  rejected above on correctness surface. (b) Guest-side peek *caching* (answer empty from a
  microsecond-stale cached verdict without touching shm) — rejected: it creates a window in
  which a posted message is invisibly missed, i.e. it converts the one-sided hazard from
  "bounded by a live shm read" to "bounded by a timer," which is exactly the silent-wrong-
  answer class this project treats as worse than failure; and the live shm read it would
  avoid is one mostly-read-shared cache line, written by wineserver only on state change —
  there is nothing worth caching. (c) Cheapening the trap itself (report item 2) is
  orthogonal and keeps 100% of calls correct, but its ceiling is a fraction of 1.4 µs
  per call versus ~98% of it for a served fast path; it remains the follow-on, not the
  substitute.
* The existing mechanism already is the right restructure: the whole empty-peek service
  moved into guest user space, gated by the same predicate native uses, with a forced-trap
  heartbeat for the side effects. Its only defect is that its admission test is narrower
  than the native predicate it mirrors. The fix is to widen the admission test to the full
  native predicate — after which guest and native agree *by construction* on every answer
  the guest gives.

**The generalized predicate**, computed guest-side from the call's own arguments, mirroring
`peek_message()` (`message.c:2966`) line for line, constants from `include/winuser.h`:

```
signal = HIWORD(flags);  if (signal == 0) signal = 0x1cff        # QS_ALLINPUT
clear  = 0
if signal & 0x0008:            # QS_POSTMESSAGE
    clear |= 0x0098            # QS_POSTMESSAGE|QS_HOTKEY|QS_TIMER
    if first == 0 && last == 0:  clear |= 0x0100   # QS_ALLPOSTMESSAGE (peek_message
                                                   # rewrites last=~0; first==last==0 is
                                                   # the equivalent pre-rewrite test)
if signal & 0x1c07:  clear |= 0x1c07               # QS_INPUT
if signal & 0x0020:  clear |= 0x0020               # QS_PAINT
if signal & 0x0400:  signal |= 0x0007              # QS_RAWINPUT pulls key+mouse classes

EMPTY  iff  shm.wake_mask == 0 && shm.changed_mask == 0
        &&  (shm.wake_bits & signal) == 0
        &&  (shm.changed_bits & clear) == 0        # both read under the seqlock, as today
```

`hwnd`, `first`, `last` no longer refuse admission (per A.3 they don't affect emptiness);
`first`/`last` participate only in the `QS_ALLPOSTMESSAGE` clear-bit. Everything else is
unchanged from the shipped body: the seed check and sabotage tag, the tick/256-budget forced
trap, the seqlock protocol (even seq → fields → seq unchanged, TSO ordering preserved by the
emulator), fall-to-trap on any failure, `%rcx` untouched before commit.

Size estimate: the current body is ~25 instructions; the predicate above adds roughly 15–20
(HIWORD extract, three conditional or-masks, one range test) — well under the checker's
96-byte fallback-scan window concern since the fallback `jmp` already sits first. The
`spec2thunk-check` third-spelling cross-check and the layout C_ASSERT discipline extend to
the new constants (0x1cff/0x1dbf stay; 0x0098/0x1c07/0x0020/0x0007/0x0100 join them).

**Deliberately preserved divergences from a real native empty peek** (same list and same
justification as the shipped body — restated because the generalization widens how often
they apply): no `check_for_driver_events` (heartbeat covers it at ≥64 Hz per thread via the
tick force, plus ~33/frame budget-forced at observed rates), no `flush_window_surfaces`
(same heartbeat), no `NtYieldExecution` (a Wine kindness, not a Windows contract), no
`access_time` 3-second guard (the forced real peek self-heals it far inside 3 s — and the
native path it lands on still runs the guard).

**Sent messages.** With no `PM_QS_*` word, `signal` includes `QS_SENDMESSAGE` (0x40 ∈
0x1cff), so a pending cross-thread `SendMessage` forces the trap and gets dispatched —
unchanged. With `PM_QS_INPUT` etc., `signal` excludes it — and that mirrors native
`check_queue_bits` exactly, which also skips the server with a sent message pending when the
filter excludes `QS_SENDMESSAGE`. Whatever the Windows-fidelity status of that corner, the
guest gives the same answer native gives; no *new* behavior is introduced. That is the
design's correctness invariant, stated once and testable: **for every call the fast body
answers, the native path would have produced the same answer without a server round trip.**

## A.5 Correctness under races — what optimism is allowed

The linearization argument: the seqlock read yields a snapshot consistent at some instant
during the call. If the snapshot satisfies the predicate, native `check_queue_bits` called at
that instant returns skip → `PeekMessage` returns FALSE. `PeekMessage`'s contract is
point-in-time; a message posted one cycle after the snapshot is indistinguishable from one
posted one cycle after a native call returned. **Safe to answer optimistically: "empty" from
a seqlock-consistent quiet snapshot. Everything else — odd seq, moved seq, any nonzero
tested bit or mask, unseeded slot, exhausted budget, moved tick — must trap.** The next
poll reads *live* shm, so a posted message is seen within one poll interval (~4 µs at the
observed rate): the wrongness window of an optimistic empty is bounded by the arrival of the
very next call, which is the same bound the native skip path has.

Why a wrong "empty" is a correctness bug, not a slow frame — the three concrete failure
shapes, which are exactly what the sabotage mode manufactures on purpose:

1. **Starvation → hang.** A thread blocked in cross-thread `SendMessage` waits for the
   receiver to dispatch; if the receiver's polls answer empty while `QS_SENDMESSAGE` is set
   (a broken mask), the sender hangs forever. Deadlock, not latency.
2. **Input death.** Driver events (X11) do not set queue bits until pumped; only real peeks
   pump. Break the heartbeat and the game stops seeing the keyboard — permanently, silently.
3. **Ordering/duplication** — structurally impossible here (empty-only, A.3), *provided* the
   body never writes anything but its own TEB scratch. The design keeps the body store-free
   except gs:0x1a0/0x1a4.

Residual race-class risks and their standing answers:

* **Torn shm read** — seqlock protocol, identical to the native reader's; any tear traps.
* **Stale queue pointer** — the shared object is cached per-thread for thread lifetime by
  `get_shared_queue`; the session mapping outlives the thread. Same lifetime argument the
  shipped body relies on; unchanged.
* **Wrong constant / drift against Wine upstream** — the predicate constants duplicate
  `peek_message()`'s. Mitigation: the `spec2thunk-check` cross-spelling, plus one gate case
  per constant (A.6). Residual risk: an upstream Wine rebase changes `peek_message`'s
  bit computation and the guest copy silently diverges. Named, accepted, and fenced by the
  gate running in CI-equivalent (`gate-suite.sh`).
* **FEX memory-model** — x86 TSO load ordering is preserved by the emulator (the shipped
  body already depends on this; the ecs/lcs bodies depend harder).

## A.6 Proof — distinguishing a correct fast path from a silently wrong one

Layered, extending the existing gate rather than replacing it
(`check-peek-fastpath.sh` + `probes/peek_fastpath.c` keep their five layers; new cases slot
into the same PASS/FAIL one-line discipline, exit = failure count):

1. **Per-shape delivery cases** (new probe legs, one per admitted shape class):
   ranged filter `(WM_APP, WM_APP)`, hwnd-filtered (a real created window), `PM_QS_INPUT`,
   `PM_QS_POSTMESSAGE`, `PM_NOREMOVE`. Each: post a matching message → must arrive on the
   next poll; post a *non-matching* message → must NOT arrive on that shape but must still
   arrive on the null filter afterward (no swallow); `PM_NOREMOVE` → same message visible
   twice then removable (no phantom removal).
2. **Sent-message liveness**: a second guest thread does `SendMessageTimeout` at the polling
   thread's window while it polls null-filter; the call must complete within the timeout.
   This is the deadlock class made loud.
3. **Sabotage negative control, per shape**: under `WINE_PPC64LE_PEEK_SABOTAGE=1` each new
   shape must STARVE (proves the *guest body*, not the trap, is what answered that shape —
   the gate's existing logic, widened), and the kill switch on top must un-starve it.
4. **Differential exactly-once fuzz** (new, the strongest layer): producer thread posts a
   randomized message stream (types across ranges, random µs gaps); consumer runs randomized
   admitted shapes. Every posted message carries a serial in wParam. Accounting: each serial
   delivered exactly once (for PM_REMOVE legs), within a deadline, in per-type order.
   Run the identical seed twice — `WINE_PPC64LE_NO_PEEK_BYPASS=1` (trap-only reference) and
   fast path on — and both must satisfy the same invariants. Any miss/duplicate/deadline
   prints the shape and serial and fails loudly. (Full transcript equality across lanes is
   NOT required — legal interleavings differ; the invariants are the contract.)
5. **In-game validation**: one benchmark leg with `WINE_PPC64LE_TRAP_STATS`: the
   `PeekMessageW` row must crater (predicted: 247.8k/s → low thousands/s = forced heartbeat
   traps + message-returning peeks + mask-healing traps). Input must demonstrably work
   (owner: menu + rebind a key + drive). A cratered count with working input is the
   end-to-end proof; a cratered count with dead input is failure mode 2 caught before it
   ships.

## A.7 The poll-idiom question — is 16,600/frame load-bearing burn or elastic spin?

Evidence in hand:

* **For "real burn":** GameThread on-CPU 87.6%; and [MEASURED today] peeks are *interleaved*
  with guest work at sub-ms granularity — no multi-ms spin phase exists (run-length data in
  the header). If the game interleaves peeks with real work (a job/fiber scheduler that
  pumps per iteration is the classic idiom), every peek's 1.4 µs sits on the critical path:
  work is delayed by exactly the peek tax.
* **For "elastic spin":** the per-*second* rate is phase-constant (203k/s loading, 248k/s
  flythrough) — the signature of a loop that runs as fast as the CPU allows rather than a
  per-frame-bounded pump. If those iterations are *waits* (poll until a fence/atomic flips),
  cheap peeks just mean more iterations before the same external event, and the recovered
  12 ms returns as idle, not fps.
* The two are compatible: an as-fast-as-possible scheduler loop doing real work each
  iteration is per-second-constant AND load-bearing. The 1 ms-granularity interleaving plus
  ~1.6 µs of JIT-time-per-peek-interval (14 ms JIT / 8.6k peeks) leans this way, but a
  sub-ms alternation of micro-waits and work is not excluded.

**The A/B that settles it (delay injection, no fast path needed, ~1 day incl. runs):**
an env-gated calibrated busy-wait (`WINE_PPC64LE_PEEK_DELAY_NS=X`) inserted in native
`NtUserPeekMessage` immediately before the empty return. Owner runs the benchmark at
X = 0, 2,000, 5,000. Analysis: regress median frametime (and the floor) against
X × per-frame peek count.

* Slope ≈ 1 (each added ns appears in the frame): the storm is critical-path burn;
  λ ≈ 1; the fast path's recovery estimate stands. The slope also *predicts* the win by
  extrapolating to −1,400 ns — a measured forecast before any guest assembly is written.
* Slope ≈ 0: elastic spin. The 12 ms is real CPU but fake frametime; serving peeks fast
  buys single-digit-% fps at best (SMT4 relief for sibling threads, nothing more). What
  changes: the fast path drops to "nice to have" (cheap, still worth landing for CPU/power
  and because a spin that iterates 8× faster finds its flipped flag ~0.7 µs sooner), and
  the investigation pivots to *what the loop is waiting on* — off-CPU/wakeup analysis of
  the GameThread (`perf sched` / wakeup tracing) becomes the top item, because the true
  bound is then some other thread this budget never profiled.
* Intermediate slope λ: recovery scales by λ; both conclusions in proportion.

Caveat, labeled: delay injection probes the +direction derivative; a loop bounded by
iteration *count* rather than time could respond asymmetrically. Accepted — the fast path
behind its env var is itself the definitive A/B (step 5 of A.6) once built, and the delay
experiment's job is only to decide whether to build it *first*.

## A.8 Expected recovery, confidence, risks

Arithmetic (all inputs measured): 8,600 peeks per 34.7 ms floor frame × ~1.4 µs all-in
(1.0–1.2 µs machinery + ~0.4 µs native win32u, incl. the two thunk-lock user-mode callbacks,
the yield, and the SEQ_CST-heavy `get_tick_count` — all of which vanish with the syscall) ≈
12 ms. Post-fix residual with serve fraction f: `(1−f)·8.6k·1.4µs + f·8.6k·~25ns + ~35
forced traps·1.4µs` (the ~25 ns fast-body cost is a **guess** — ~40 instructions + one
shared cache line; the probe's fastness layer will measure it). At f = 0.95: residual
~0.9 ms → **recovers ~11 ms of the 12**; at f = 0.75 → ~8.5 ms.

Scaled by the A/B's λ and applied proportionally (peek rate is per-second-constant, so the
tax is a ~34% share of *every* frame, not a fixed 12 ms of the floor only):

* λ≈1, f≥0.9: floor 34.7 → ~24 ms, median 51.8 → ~38–41 ms, **avg ~18.5 → ~24–27 fps**.
* λ≈0.5: avg → ~21–22 fps. λ≈0: avg ~19 fps (recovered CPU, not frames).

**Confidence: medium.** The per-peek cost and count are measured (high); f is unmeasured
until the A.2 capture (H-state or a large message fraction could gut it); λ is unmeasured
until the A.7 delay runs. Both gates are cheap and come before the build.

Main ways it goes wrong, ranked:

1. **λ ≈ 0** (elastic spin) — the whole 12 ms is real CPU but not real frametime. Caught by
   a 1-day experiment before implementation. This is the biggest expected-value risk.
2. **f small for state reasons** — a stuck queue bit or perpetual mask churn defeats any
   admission test. Caught by the A.2 capture; the fix then lives elsewhere (the bit's
   source), not in this design.
3. **Input death / SendMessage starvation shipped silently** — the catastrophic failure.
   Fenced by: one-sided error surface (A.3), heartbeat preserved unchanged (A.4), per-shape
   sabotage controls and the liveness/fuzz layers (A.6), manual input validation (A.6.5).
4. **Constant drift on a future Wine rebase** — fenced by gate-in-CI, named as residual.

Effort: capture ½ day; delay A/B 1 day; generalized body + checker + probe legs 2–3 days;
in-game validation 1 day. All in the Wine tree — **zero FEXCore coordination cost.**

# Part B — The ~14 ms of JIT'd guest code: measurement specification

Per the scope change: no fix design. The 14 ms is one number with nothing behind it; the
spec below is what turns it into a decision. Four measurements, ordered; each names its
commands, its outcomes, and what each outcome implies. All are owner-run (they need game
launches); analysis is scriptable afterward from the artifacts.

Standard env for every leg (memory-verified): the FEX_* quad
(`FEX_APP_DATA_LOCATION`, `FEX_ROOTFS`, `FEX_THUNKGUESTLIBS`, `FEX_THUNKHOSTLIBS`) +
`WINE_PPC64LE_TREE=$HOME/Development/powerpc64le-ports/hangover-ppc64le/wine-build`.

## B.1 Verified per-block attribution of the 37.6% (the core measurement)

The 0830 leg's JIT samples are all `[unknown]` because the game's perf map was empty
(header note). Re-run with the map gated:

```
# owner launches the benchmark with FEX_LIBRARYJITNAMING=1 in the app env
# find the game: GAMEPID such that comm==GameThread and tid==pid (threads.comm recipe)
test -s /tmp/perf-$GAMEPID.map || echo "ABORT: map empty — naming not armed, do not capture"
perf record -t $GAMEPID -F 999 -o perf-jit.data -- sleep 15        # same window as 0830
cp /tmp/perf-$GAMEPID.map .                                        # BEFORE process exit
perf report -i perf-jit.data --sort symbol --percent-limit 0.05 > jit-report.txt
```

Map entries are `JIT_0x<guestRIP>_<hostaddr>` (format verified against
`/tmp/perf-1251408.map`, 31k entries). Post-processing: bucket guest RIPs by module using
the PE load addresses from the run's wine log (the port logs module mappings; else one
`WINEDEBUG=+module` line-capture leg), classifying into
{Cyberpunk2077.exe, guest system DLLs, thunk DLLs, JIT trampolines/dispatcher}.

Outcomes:
* **Concentrated** (top ~20 blocks > 30% of JIT time): hot-loop shaped → go to B.3 on those
  blocks; codegen quality is decidable per-block.
* **Flat** (top 20 < 10%): broad real work → per-block codegen matters less than global
  effects; B.2's icache/branch data carries the verdict.
* **Material non-game-module share** (guest-side d3d12/thunk/dispatcher blocks > ~15%):
  the "game's own work" label on the 14 ms is partly wrong and that share belongs to the
  crossing story of Part A / report item 2 instead.

## B.2 Machine-level quality: IPC and front-end behavior, thread-scoped

```
perf list 2>/dev/null | grep -iE "PM_(BR_MPRED|L1_ICACHE|INST_FROM|ICT)" | head   # exact names on this kernel
perf stat -t $GAMEPID -e cycles,instructions,PM_BR_MPRED_CMPL,PM_L1_ICACHE_MISS,PM_INST_FROM_L3MISS -- sleep 15
```

Control: the same counters around `probes/guest/com_crossing_cost.c`'s guest-local loop
(loop 1, the 6.4 ns/call leg) — that is this JIT's best-case IPC on this core, measured on
the same silicon.

Interpretation rule: if in-game thread IPC is within ~2x of the probe loop's, the JIT is
executing about as well in the large as in the small and the 14 ms is ~this game's real cost
under this JIT (icache pressure did not materialize) → *largely irreducible without FEXCore
codegen work*. If IPC is ≥3–4x worse and `PM_L1_ICACHE_MISS`/`PM_INST_FROM_L3MISS` are the
divergent counters, the microbenchmark was hiding front-end starvation — code expansion and
block layout are the lever, which IS FEXCore territory (block placement, code cache
locality), i.e. co-developer coordination before any effort is spent.
(The 2x/4x thresholds are judgment lines, not measured facts — labeled as such.)

## B.3 Expansion-ratio spot check on the top blocks (only if B.1 is concentrated)

For each of the top ~5 blocks: guest bytes from the exe
(`guestRIP − module_base + PE section offset`, objdump -D -b binary -m i386:x86-64), host

FEX-class JITs typically land ~2–3x instruction expansion on integer code (**guess** —
rule-of-thumb, not measured here); sustained >5x with heavy spill traffic on hot blocks is

to take to the co-developer.

## B.4 The emulated-Proton-lane comparison — what it does and does not control

Both lanes run the **same FEXCore JIT**, so this comparison says nothing about codegen
quality. What it isolates is the *crossing architecture's second-order tax on guest code*:
our lane crosses 1.579 M/s (host machinery churning caches/BTB against JIT'd code ~55k times
per floor frame); the Proton lane keeps Wine itself as guest code and crosses only for real
syscalls. Same game, same JIT, radically different crossing rate.

Fair-run protocol: same benchmark, same 4K Medium FSR2 settings, same machine otherwise
idle; `FEX_LIBRARYJITNAMING=1` in both lanes (same perturbation); tid-scoped 999 Hz capture
of the *game's main thread* in both; B.1's module bucketing on both; compare
**game-module-attributed JIT ms per frame** (normalize by each lane's median frametime —
the lanes will not run at the same fps, and Proton-lane total JIT time includes guest-Wine,
which must be excluded, or the comparison is unsound).

Outcomes:
* Game-module JIT ms/frame ≈ equal (within ~20%): the crossing storm is not inflating guest
  code execution; the 14 ms is what CP2077 costs under FEX on this machine. Combined with a
  clean B.2, the honest conclusion is **this line is the wall for now** — and 14 ms of
  genuine work is a 71 fps ceiling by itself, so the port's remaining wins are Part A and
  report item 2, full stop.
* Ours materially higher (≥1.5x): our own lane inflates JIT execution (cache/BTB eviction by
  trap machinery, block unlinking around crossings) — good news, because that is *our* tree
  and our crossing design, no FEXCore coordination, and it compounds with Part A (fewer
  crossings = less eviction).

Cost of all of B: two benchmark legs + one probe counter run + a gdb session ≈ half a day of
machine time, analysis a day.

# Part C — Sequencing and the honest ceiling

Order (dependency-driven, cheapest decisive measurement first):

1. **A.2 shape/state capture** (½ day) — gates everything in A; also step 1 of the report's
   own plan.
2. **A.7 delay-injection A/B** (1 day) — decides λ before any guest assembly exists. Can
   share a single temporary win32u build with A.2's instrumentation.
3. **B.1 + B.2 legs** (½ day machine time) — independent of 1–2; can interleave on the same
   days. They cost nothing but runs and settle whether B is a wall or a lever.
4. **If f·λ from 1–2 is healthy (≥~0.6): build A.4 + A.6** (2–3 days, Wine tree only).
   If λ ≈ 0: skip to the off-CPU/wakeup investigation A.7 names, and reprioritize report
   item 2 downward too (a spin pays trap costs that also don't buy frames).
5. **B.3/B.4 only if B.1/B.2 point at codegen or crossing-tax** — B.4 also becomes more
   informative *after* A lands (A removes most of the crossing storm, so re-running the
   lane comparison then directly measures the residual tax).
6. Report item 2 (trap-return shortening, fexbridge state-sync micro-profile,
   `get_tick_count`'s SEQ_CST loads — noted in passing: on POWER9 those
   `__ATOMIC_SEQ_CST` loads in `message.c:70` are hwsync-bearing and 3.8% of the thread,
   but most of that money is inside the peek path and dies with A anyway) — after A's
   results, informed by its A/B, exactly as the report already sequenced it.

**The ceiling, plainly.** If A lands at f≥0.9 and λ≈1: avg ~18.5 → ~24–27 fps, floor
34.7 → ~24 ms. If report item 2 then shaves ~30% off the remaining ~5–6 ms of non-peek
machinery: high-20s. **Neither path reaches 60 fps, and if B.2/B.4 return "the 14 ms is
real," ~71 fps is the hard ceiling with a perfect port of everything else — realistically
this work gets Cyberpunk from 18.5 to the mid-to-high 20s at 4K Medium, and 60 is not on
this road.** The one scenario that moves the wall itself is B.4's second outcome (our
crossings inflate guest code), which is why its cheap measurement belongs in the first
week's batch despite B carrying no fix design today.
